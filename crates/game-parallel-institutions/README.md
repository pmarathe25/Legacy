# Parallel institutions

Data-defined regional bodies have members, an independently held office, quorum, and an open or closed trade law. A local member opens one ballot per body, eligible local members vote explicitly, and a majority meeting quorum resolves at its deadline. Elections and law changes are separate from the reincarnation lineage; a ruler gains no office automatically. A vacant office is not magically filled.

A ballot records a declared trade rule; it does not prevent people from trading. A rule has practical force only through people's choices and concrete actions. The current voting and membership representation is a limited model, not an implementation of emergent institutions, coercion, or enforcement.

Members may leave voluntarily. Their office and pending votes are removed, and their own proposals or candidacies are cancelled. A body below its normal quorum becomes inactive; its remaining members can admit people to restore quorum. A body with no members remains as history while a new local body may be founded.

When a member dies, lifecycle publication removes that person from membership and office and cancels their open proposals or candidacies. Votes from deceased members are withdrawn; if a body loses all members, pending applications receive a recorded rejection and the empty body remains as history.
