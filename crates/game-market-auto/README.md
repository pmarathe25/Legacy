# autonomous market

Opted-in recipients rank feasible pending offers by their own valuations and social incentives. The recipient chooses the greatest surplus, with offer ID resolving equal scores. Competing chosen exchanges that use the same person's custody enter deterministic arbitration. An unselected offer remains available for later consideration or withdrawal. Missing valuations prevent acceptance, and equal value is accepted when minimum gain is zero.

A policy with `propose = true` also lets its person propose trades using their valuations and visible local carried goods. A proposal does not transfer goods: its recipient independently evaluates it at a later boundary. Missing valuations prevent proposals, and competing participants can defer each other under the conservative priority rule.

An offer can also be a one-sided request for a gift. A person with `request_help = true` may autonomously ask a local holder for a valued item, without offering anything in return. A recipient's `help_value` expresses their personal value for helping, while optional directed `influences` add subjective attachment, obligation and fear to their evaluation of the requester. These are personal estimates, not proof of approval, a threat, or an enforceable duty; a record that an exchange was accepted means that the physical action occurred, not that it was freely approved. An indifferent person can leave the same request unanswered. When `opinions.rules.gratitude_gain` is configured, directly receiving a gift can increase the recipient's felt obligation toward the giver. The opinions owner separately learns fear from actual harm when `opinions.rules.fear_per_damage` is configured; uninjured bystanders do not automatically learn it.

The composed economy matches proposals by local endpoint and checks proximity again before accepting and transferring goods. The optional market workload in the [mixed campaign benchmark](../game-economy-cli/README.md) exercises requests and completed exchanges across worker counts.
