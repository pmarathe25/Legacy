# Physical confinement

This owner models doors attached to real local sites, people held behind them, and each captive's own inclination and effort to escape. The economic host stages these actions with the rest of the world, blocks departures through intact barriers, and frees occupants when a barrier breaks or its site is destroyed. A person can attempt capture without the target's consent; a conscious target may yield willingly or from their own fear or felt obligation, and a stronger actor can overpower resistance. A target already engaged in another action is unavailable that boundary. A player can open a cell or attempt to escape. People can recover while held, and autonomous captives can independently attempt escape. The records do not imply authority, employment, or an institution.

Scenarios supply existing physical sites, their barrier resistance, occupants, and individual escape preferences. `cell_kinds` assigns initial resistance to matching sites only when real construction succeeds using its required material. `default_effort` sets the fallback strength and defaults to one; autonomous escape attempts require an individual preference. Optional `capture_responses` set each target's `yield_willingly` and `pressure_threshold`; absent preferences do not force a person to feel pressure. Individual `capture_policies` specify a person, target, site, willingness, and optional `pressured_by` and `pressure_threshold`; a person can decline unless their own fear or felt obligation motivates them. Concurrent attempts reserve disjoint people and finite cell capacity, and an explicit player action has priority. Individual `guard_policies` similarly express a person's willingness or personal pressure to guard a site. A guard must actually be present and available, spends their action, and opposes escape using their own effort. Sites used as cells cannot simultaneously serve a production recipe. The current model treats the cell as a barrier to outside actions; carried food and personal treatment remain available.

`cargo test --release -p game-economy-confinement concentrated_escape_benchmark -- --ignored --nocapture` measures simultaneous attempts against one shared barrier; `concentrated_capture_benchmark` measures many people attempting to capture one target, `concentrated_release_benchmark` measures shared and distinct release targets, and `concentrated_guarding_benchmark` combines guards and captives. Set `LEGACY_PEOPLE` and `LEGACY_WORKERS` to choose the workload and worker count.

Set `LEGACY_CONFINED=1` when running `cargo test --release -p game-economy concentrated_giving_benchmark -- --ignored --nocapture` to measure many captives giving into one cellmate's finite carrying capacity. The same option on `concentrated_family_care_benchmark` measures caregivers and dependents inside one shared cell.

An actual newborn remains in the mother's cell if she is still held when the birth is staged. `born_inside` records that physical placement, which can overcrowd an already full cell; all occupants still count when deciding whether another capture can be admitted. A birth is not treated as a capture or a consent decision. `concentrated_birth_placement_benchmark` measures parallel placement of many newborns.

The family owner receives the cell partition at the start and end of each boundary. Its in-person matching and conception require the people to share a partition throughout that boundary; an opening used to pass supplies does not permit physical contact through the barrier.

Optional `release_policies` express individual intentions to free a particular person. An available, capable person at the cell can act from willingness or their own fear or obligation toward `pressured_by`; competing releases select one actor deterministically. The action consumes the actor's time, including when resisted. A guard may personally choose `oppose_opening` to resist outsiders as well as escapes; the default is false. Escape-only guards' effort is allocated to escape first, followed by flexible guards' effort, and any remaining flexible effort opposes openings. Controlled opening takes priority over autonomous openings, whose remaining opposition is allocated deterministically across attempts. Effort is spent only once. Administrative `guard-opening PERSON on|off` changes this preference, and `release-choice PERSON on|off` changes release willingness without eliminating personal pressure.

An existing cell or a constructed `cell_kinds` entry can set `allow_supplies = true` to represent an opening through which possessions and food requests can pass. A captive may ask for food or give possessions through that opening, or to another occupant of the same cell, if they are not occupied by an escape attempt, personal treatment, or another chosen action. The recipient decides independently whether to respond. Gifts and promised deliveries from outside require the opening while the recipient is held, and ordinary transfers still check actual custody and carrying capacity. A caregiver in the same cell can provide dependent care and use carried food, but cannot fetch stock outside the barrier. An opening does not permit a person to leave or provide in-person care through the barrier.
