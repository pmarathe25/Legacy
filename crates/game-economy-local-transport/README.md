# Local stores and carrying

Distinct named stores have their own capacity and dated physical custody. Individuals can choose to pick up goods and deposit them at another store or regional stock on separate boundaries. The actor retains custody between actions and may stop, while physical transactions resolve competing uses and preserve expiry. Fear or felt obligation may independently motivate a carrier; no organization or agreement compels them.

Optional individual goal-fetching preferences allow people to seek missing inputs for their own active work goals in observed stores in their current region. They carry and deposit the actual goods across separate boundaries, can stop at any time, and do not gain knowledge of remote stores. Existing direct work choices take precedence.

When personal input planning is enabled, the search also follows bounded recipe dependencies to locate supplies for intermediate work. Cycles and the number of alternatives explored per person are bounded, and available alternatives rotate between reviews.

An optional `walking` model adds directed, data-defined travel times between regional stock and named stores. A person has an individual position or a paused walking leg; goods stay in their actual carried custody while they walk. A carrying preference can independently motivate walking to its source and destination, and the transfer requires a later boundary after arrival. Changing willingness can pause the leg. This first logistics slice conservatively pauses many autonomous discretionary activities while the person is away from stock; local carrying, food pickup from the current store, reading or writing carried pages, and encounters remain available; it does not yet model street-level social proximity or automatically route goal-fetching, food seeking, or expeditions to stores. Those planners cannot inspect or take from remote named stores while this model is enabled. Regional departure requires reaching the stock node.

The ignored `local_walking_and_contended_pickup_benchmark` exercises a full boundary for 100,000 people and a subsequent shared-store pickup. On a Ryzen 5 7600, one and six workers measured 46.0 and 17.6 ms for walking, and 136.1 and 53.5 ms for pickup; both runs produced the same state fingerprint. These are workload-specific measurements, not a linear-scaling guarantee.
