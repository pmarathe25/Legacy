# Personal practices

This owner stores bounded individual experiences and individually held proposals. People construct and communicate conditional actions from experiences; no shared institution or agreement compels a participant. Hosts supply actual observations and encounters and must execute physical actions separately.

Readiness reports record only a speaker's claim, not privileged inventory truth. A recipient may separately believe, doubt, or leave the statement unassessed. Reports require contact and never make physical goods available.

A recipient may also choose a personal trust threshold for readiness reports. At assessment, they compare their own directed trust in that speaker with the threshold and believe or disbelieve accordingly; other people's trust and the actual stock do not decide their belief. The economy supplies existing personal trust, including its configured initial value for an unfamiliar speaker. `practice-attitude PERSON min THRESHOLD` sets this choice in the economy CLI.

An optional `value_learning` on a personal mind holds a bounded attachment to conditional food aid. Its signed `received`, `gave`, and `unanswered` changes describe that person's own interpretations; absent learning leaves the prior behavior unchanged. A received gift or unanswered request is associated with an active proposal the person authored to that counterpart; a gift the person gives is associated with a known proposal from that recipient. Matching gifts also use the proposal's good. This is the person's modeled association, not proof of another person's motive or dishonesty. Giving under pressure can still change the giver's own value, without implying approval.

The committed value enters that person's score for later proposals. Newly observed experiences affect decisions starting at a later boundary; positive, negative, and zero changes are all possible. The value does not compel adoption or delivery, create an obligation, or change anyone else's mind. It is bounded by the individual's configured minimum and maximum, saved with that mind, and copied only when changed.

Optional `precedent` reasoning compares a new proposal with at most the person's bounded remembered cases. Signed `same_person`, `same_good`, and `quantity_difference` weights select the case most relevant to that individual; a negative difference weight favors closer quantities, while a positive one favors larger differences. The person's independent `received`, `gave`, `unanswered`, or `shortage` interpretation of that case contributes to their proposal score. Ties use deterministic case ordering. No cases means no precedent contribution. This is a narrow food-aid model, not unrestricted reasoning over arbitrary principles.

Optional signed `proposal_affinity_weight` adds the recipient's directed affinity for the author to their own approval score. It can make liking or disliking someone matter in either direction, or have no effect. Fear and felt obligation remain separate: someone may outwardly adopt a proposal they privately disapprove of, and neither adoption nor approval compels later action.

An optional personal `work` mind can construct a work-and-goods proposal from an active goal, a personally witnessed successful requested job, and a remembered gift of any known good. Each person retains at most 16 directly experienced gifts; these observations enter later decisions and do not imply any particular attitude toward the other person. A controlled person can also propose any known good directly. Terms are communicated only during contact; the worker decides later whether to adopt them. An author with `ask_willing` may make an ordinary local work request, while an adopting worker may value the received terms in their own work preference. Terms are claims, so neither adoption nor requesting creates goods or guarantees a transfer.

An optional `work.evaluation` scores a proposal using that recipient's own directed affinity and trust toward its author, with signed weights and a personal threshold. Either relationship can matter positively, negatively, or not at all. When no evaluation is configured, the person's `approve` setting determines private approval. Fear and felt obligation may separately cause outward adoption without private approval.

An optional `work.outcome_policy` lets a worker form an expectation from terms they received and adopted before starting the actual requested job. They retain the specific terms and job, including if the requester is absent when it finishes. Directly received matching gifts are personally attributed to the oldest matching expectation, one unit at a time; this attribution can be mistaken. After their own `wait_ticks`, if they have not attributed the full quantity, their configured signed `missing_trust` and `missing_affinity` changes are applied once. Either change can be positive, negative, or zero; omitting the policy creates no expectation or reaction. The record describes the worker's perspective and does not determine whether anyone lied, owed anything, or had the resources to give.

The author remembers one admitted request and can later choose a gift after personally witnessing that specific job finish. Their current `willing`, `reserve`, and pressure threshold govern autonomous decisions; local contact, real stock and capacity are still required. They may personally associate matching accepted gifts after that observation with their intention, including several partial gifts. Once the remembered quantity is reached, they clear the intention; failed transfers do not count. An old unfulfilled request is eventually forgotten after the proposal expires plus its configured lifetime; this bounds personal memory rather than enforcing a contract.
