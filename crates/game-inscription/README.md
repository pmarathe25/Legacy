# game-inscription

Owns physical mark-making independently of language, scripts, and literary genres.
Any authored object with `mark-surface:N` is a surface of hardness N. Pigments
(`mark-pigment`) can mark it, or an implement with `mark-cutting:N` can incise a
surface no harder than itself. `mark-impressible` permits finger impressions.
Examples include stone, slate, wood, bark, fabric, clay, and paper; charcoal,
ochre, dyes, ink, and cutting tools provide different methods.

`supplies` finds a local unmarked physical unit and a compatible method. A tool
may be carried separately. `Make` checks ability and locality, records actual
property use, consumes a pigment portion when needed, and identifies the original
surface through `game-items`. Incision tools remain reusable. Surface goods keep
their original mass, material, and custody. No paper page is manufactured from
stone or cloth. Existing heirloom identities are preserved; already-inscribed
units are not silently overwritten.

Callers charge time using the returned method factor. Literacy and creative
pastimes recheck the physical inscription at completion; interrupted work can
leave unfinished marks. Movement and destruction follow ordinary item/cargo rules.
The model covers represented material surfaces, not unrepresented geometric walls.

Literacy tests exercise stone carving, charcoal on fabric, clay impressions,
regional materials, missing tools, rollback, and save/load.
