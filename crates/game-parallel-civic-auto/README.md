# Autonomous civic decisions

Opted-in people can vote on local open ballots according to authored trade preferences and their own directed trust in a candidate. A person can abstain, can cast at most one automatic vote per tick, and cannot replace an existing vote. Votes use ordinary institutional validation and reserve the person's action time. Actual ballots, offices, and laws remain owned by the institutions module.
The runnable `content/parallel/civic_auto.toml` fixture includes two citizens who prefer closed trade. Policies abstain on unspecified topics; election preferences use directed personal trust (or neutral zero when opinions are disabled).
Optional `propose_trade = true` and `proposal_duration` let a person initiate a ballot when the institution has no open ballot and its current trade law conflicts with that person's preference. The proposal itself consumes the person's action, and the decision still requires actual votes and quorum. See `content/parallel/civic_proposals.toml`.
A policy can instead specify `trade_trust_target` and `trade_trust_threshold`: the citizen supports open trade while their own directed trust in that person meets the threshold. Agreement outcomes can therefore change that citizen's later proposal or vote. This is an authored personal inference, not universal access to others' experiences.

When `[civic_auto.founding]` specifies `minimum_founders` and `quorum`, enabled local proponents with a trade preference can voluntarily establish a body in a region that has none. Available proponents become its members, select the initial trade policy by majority (ties favor open trade), and use the normal ballot and market rules thereafter. The founding decision is staged with the tick, reserves its members, and groups independent regions in parallel. This is a bounded founding mechanism; it does not yet implement general institutional invention, competing jurisdictions, or enforcement.

Local people may apply for membership; members with `minimum_candidate_trust` vote according to their own directed trust in the applicant. Available members are partitioned into independent groups that each meet quorum, so multiple applications can be decided concurrently while each voter participates once per tick. Applications remain pending when no quorum is available. Accepted and rejected decisions survive save/load.

An enabled policy may set `leave_on_trade_disagreement = true` to make that citizen leave a local institution whose current trade rule conflicts with their own preference. The decision reserves that person for the tick, and departures from independent institutions and concentrated memberships are processed in parallel. See `content/parallel/resignation.toml`.

An enabled `seek_membership = true` policy lets a local person apply to an institution whose trade rule is compatible with their own preference. Applications are real, persistent requests and require a later decision by existing members; they are rejected if the institution simultaneously loses all its members. See `content/parallel/joining.toml`.

Members can cast an explicit `membership-vote BODY APPLICANT PERSON yes|no`. Recorded votes survive save/load, are combined with distinct automatic voters, and are removed if the voter leaves or dies. The ruler sees pending applications and their own vote; applicants do not see individual votes.
