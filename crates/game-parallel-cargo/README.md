# Parallel cargo state owner

Owns serializable person-held food and validates identity, quantity and capacity. The runtime composes physical actions and travel; see the parallel runtime README for gameplay limits.

`auto_eat_local` lets a hungry local person consume one meal from food they actually carry before regional food is allocated. A person who eats carried food does not also claim a regional meal that tick, even if still hungry. Carried food is decremented only when the tick commits. The setting defaults to false; the fused host supports it and the comparison hosts reject it rather than omit it. `content/parallel/local_food.toml` provides an example.

`personal_food_access` is an experimental alternative to automatic regional meals. Only people listed in `acquire_when_hungry` attempt to acquire a meal from their physically accessible local regional stock. An attempt takes an available action slot, respects carrying capacity and scarce stock, and moves food into personal custody; eating can occur on a later tick. The fused host exposes `set_food_seeking` and `food-seeking PERSON enable|disable` to change an individual's preference. `content/parallel/personal_food.toml` is a small example. Other hosts reject the mode.

A region currently represents a single universally reachable source, and deterministic rotating priority breaks simultaneous scarcity ties. Neither barriers, individual stock claims, contested taking, nor general reprioritization of food over ongoing work is modeled here. Workers whose jobs have already stalled from hunger can seek food before resuming. The generated profile uses this mode, including the remaining simplifications.

A person can physically give held food to another living local person with `give-food GIVER RECIPIENT FOOD` or a disjoint `give_food_batch`. Food and carrying capacity are checked at the same boundary, and failed batches change nothing. The transfer is an action by the giver, not an enforced promise; direct recipients can develop their own feelings of obligation according to their sensitivities. Individual requests can be submitted explicitly or created by individual `food_askers` preferences.

Optional `food_aid` policies give a person tendencies to share with known hungry local people. Their own `help_value`, experienced fear, and felt obligation are compared with their threshold; food, capacity, availability, and the giver’s retained reserve still constrain the action. The individual can disable or re-enable the tendency with `food-aid PERSON enable|disable`. Successful aid occupies both participants for the tick and moves food only at commit. Contested participants use deterministic conservative matching; unselected attempts can be reconsidered later. This models personal aid rather than institutional authority, and it does not require the giver to privately approve the relationship. See `content/parallel/food_aid.toml`.

`request-food REQUESTER GIVER ID AMOUNT EXPIRES` records a local request, and `request_food_batch` admits many requests atomically. Each requester can have one pending request. A policy with `requires_request = true` considers help only after its named recipient asks, and still evaluates the giver’s own threshold and actual capacity. A matching delivery clears the request; otherwise it remains until its inclusive deadline passes. Neither the request nor the policy makes the giver obey.
 `food-requests PERSON` shows requests involving that person; `withdraw-food PERSON ID` lets the requester withdraw one. A matching manual gift can satisfy a request as well.

A `food_askers` entry identifies one person, a known local giver, the desired quantity, an interval, and a request lifetime. A hungry, available person can spend an action to ask when they lack a carried meal and have no pending request. The request becomes visible at commit and can be answered on a later tick; asking neither transfers food nor compels a reply.
 The person may change that tendency with `food-asking PERSON enable|disable`; existing requests can be withdrawn separately.
 The [`food_requests.toml`](../../content/parallel/food_requests.toml) fixture demonstrates an autonomous request followed by a separately chosen response.

An optional `auto_seek_age` schedules each actual newborn to gain a food-seeking preference after the specified number of ticks. Pending enrollments are saved, and an explicit personal choice cancels the scheduled change. The experimental generated profile sets this age to the end of dependent care.

`food_sharing` gives a person a general tendency to share with someone toward whom they feel sufficient fear or obligation. Candidate recipients come from that person's existing directed opinions, so new relationships can affect future behavior without a preset list of pairings. Physical locality, hunger, capacity, retained food, and competing actions are still checked. `food-aid PERSON enable|disable` controls both this general tendency and any named food-aid tendencies. The experimental generated profile seeds independent general tendencies with `food_sharing_percent`.

When an individual witnesses the completion of work they personally requested, their existing gratitude sensitivity can create a felt obligation toward the worker. A later gift is a separate, optional action and consumes real food; refusing, departing, lacking food, or changing preference can prevent it. This mechanism does not create an employment relationship or make anyone deliver compensation.

When `inherit_food_sharing` is enabled, newborns acquire a starting sharing preference from one recorded parent if that parent has one. This preference remains individually changeable.

With `choose_known = true`, a food asker can instead approach a local person toward whom they currently feel an obligation from prior help. They consider at most 32 remembered candidates and fall back to their initial contact if none is locally present; they do not know whether the candidate will respond or has food. Requests and actual gifts remain separate individual actions.

Optional `food_stores` describe distinct local stocks with their own capacities and optional personal claimants. A person's `food_store_choices` entry directs their hungry acquisition attempt to one local store; concurrent attempts compete for its real stock in a deterministic order that rotates over time. A choice is an intention, not a permission check. A claimant who sets `object_to_taking` can lose trust in someone they witness taking, but the taking still occurs. A claimant elsewhere does not automatically learn of it. Unsuccessful attempts consume an action and do not fall back to the regional source that tick; if the chosen store is in another region, the person instead uses their ordinary regional preference.

`food-stores PERSON` lists local stores, `food-source PERSON STORE|region` changes a person's source preference, and `store-take PERSON STORE AMOUNT` / `store-put PERSON STORE AMOUNT` move actual held food. Each command checks physical location and capacity. The [`food_stores.toml`](../../content/parallel/food_stores.toml) fixture demonstrates these operations. General ownership disputes and enforcement are not yet modeled; stores are enabled by the default generated profile.

An optional `food_depositors` entry gives an individual a tendency to put one carried meal into a selected local store, at their own interval and while retaining their chosen reserve. Capacity is resolved from the committed stock, so a withdrawal in the same tick does not create deposit capacity until the next tick. A successful deposit moves actual food; a refused or failed attempt cannot create stock. `food-deposit PERSON enable|disable` changes the individual's tendency. A person already occupied by another planned action cannot deposit in the same tick.

A `food_store_blueprints` entry specifies a material good, a quantity, and the resulting store capacity. `store-build PERSON STORE BLUEPRINT` constructs an empty local store only after the builder has physically acquired the required goods. Construction consumes those goods, records a personal claim by the builder, and does not prevent others from using the store. The blueprint is content data rather than a preset organization.

A claimant can change their own response to future takings with `store-objection PERSON STORE enable|disable`; changing that feeling does not change physical access.

When `reconsider_on_failure` is enabled for a person's store choice, an actual failed withdrawal causes that person to drop that intention at commit and use their regional food-seeking preference on a later tick. Successful attempts and unattempted remote stores do not trigger this change. Generated store users start with this tendency; an explicit `food-source` command creates a persistent choice instead.

Multiple people can independently assert claims on the same store with `store-claim PERSON STORE assert|withdraw`; `store-objection` controls each claimant's own response. Assertions are not exclusive, and withdrawing one person's claim does not alter anyone else's. A taking samples at most four objecting claimants, rotating deterministically by taker and tick, then only those alive and physically present can notice it. Each observer also attends to at most 32 simultaneous store takings per tick, with attention rotating over time. Thus crowded stores have bounded observation work and memory growth, and being present does not guarantee noticing every taking. These personal observations may influence later individual choices without imposing an access rule. Claims and their dispositions persist in saves.

An individual's optional `claim_after_deposit` disposition lets them assert a personal claim only after they actually deposit food. The disposition records whether they initially object to others taking; it neither grants exclusive access nor overwrites a prior claim. `deposit-claim PERSON none|accept|object` changes the future tendency, while existing claims remain individually revocable. The generated profile assigns this disposition to a configurable fraction of depositors.

A `food_store_builders` entry expresses one person's revocable intention to build a limited number of stores from a content-defined blueprint. A free, local person builds only if they hold the required material; construction consumes the material and creates an empty store with their own claim. Food seeking, eating, and already planned actions take priority. `store-building PERSON enable|disable` changes the personal intention. Optional `use_after_build` and `deposit_after_build` preferences let the person choose their new store as a future food source or deposit destination. Existing preferences can still be changed, and taking is never blocked. Construction and unrelated trade can commit in the same tick without losing either physical change.

With `inherit_store_building`, a newborn may acquire an initial personal building inclination from one recorded parent. Their own intention starts with one potential project and remains changeable; no store, claim, material, or relationship is inherited by this mechanism.
