# game-invocation

`game-invocation` defines the costs and benefits of drawing on past lives. Invocation adds burden and fatigue; continued use adds exposure. Burden bands set strain, emotional disruption, and identity effects. Sustained severe burden and exposure can cause permanent identity or lifespan loss. Rules calculate borrowed-work rates and recovery or cost thresholds.

The crate owns activation, consent, control episodes, and competing controller selection. Person-bound burdens and allocated warnings are native components in the shared store; pending consent references its warning by ID. Sparse prepared changes guard the affected rows and stage into a shared batch before control metadata is published. Invocation owns cost progression and control validation over supplied survival, incarnation, and archive facts. Accepted loss runs for its warned duration with costs continuing and dismissal unavailable. Physiology prepares instant fatigue charges; composition admits those charges and invocation changes in one batch. The application still archives actual memories and binds lifespan effects and autonomous actions while those remaining interfaces migrate. Direct identity and lifespan penalties stay with the current incarnation.

An accepted historical controller chooses typed physical intentions from its own goals and memories. Current stock and site memories override matching historical entries; current routes override recalled paths. Hunger, rest, hostility, care, and work keep their fixed priority. Physical admission can reject an intention and cause a rest attempt, without trying another target or consulting hidden stock. The host supplies body, scene, and ongoing-action facts and executes the returned intention.
