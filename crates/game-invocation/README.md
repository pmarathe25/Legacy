# game-invocation

Invocation draws on past lives' skills, goals, and memories. Activation pays fatigue and persistent burden; continued use adds exposure. Burden drives strain, emotion and identity effects. Prolonged severe exposure can permanently damage this incarnation's identity or lifespan. Historical mastery combines with current ability through owner-calculated work and combat rates.

The crate owns consent, warnings, control episodes, controller selection, costs, previews, and offered control actions. Burdens, warnings, historical archives, physical records, timed memory actions, and the invoking person's `ControlSession` are shared components. At most one session can exist; pending consent references its native warning by ID. Guarded plans stage their component changes alongside Physiology's fatigue charge in one admitted batch. Direct penalties stay with the affected incarnation. The runtime retains rules only; entity rows live only in the shared store. A `ControlReceipt` uses the generic ECS read guard to validate opening rows, session membership, and the exact admitted warning and burden projection before publication.

An accepted historical controller chooses physical intentions from its own personality and memories, with current memories taking priority. The application supplies body, scene, guidance, and ongoing-action facts. `controller_decision` joins canonical current and archived memory rows and chooses an intention; the application binds it to a physical owner. `state_view` selects player-visible control, costs, memories, and records from those same rows. `PreparedControllerReview` retains the selected archives, current mind and stocks, costs, and factual body, location, and activity reads through the final batch. Controller review prepares its subject and action from one borrowed Spatial `LocalRoster` phase before publishing the selected controller. Raw stationary roster entries preserve carried-person and road exclusions. Invocation captures each historical mind at death. Closing skill changes stage complete archive rows with guarded opening and projected values, so receiving schemas validate them before source publication. Historical content reaches Characters through its `MemoryLearner` interface or a prepared learning receipt; the recipient retains current rights, newer observations, and existing routes.

Invocation owns memory admission, deadlines, cancellation participants, completion, visibility, and whole-record transfer identity. `MemoryFacts` supplies physical contact, permissions, reservations, equipment, and carrying facts. Cargo stages writing materials and record compartment creation or movement with Invocation's native record changes in one batch. A typed start replacement preserves its new memory action while the interruption plan cancels the old shared work. Its generic read guard protects selected archives, equipment, cargo, locations, and receiving character facts through final staging. Completion and archive receipts likewise protect their opening rows and admitted projections against foreign edits.

Before publication, learning and historical skill receipts validate the final batch and reject foreign writes or removals of their receiving mind or archive. Archive finalization performs no component mutations. The assembly binds returned events and activities and reserves the required IDs and event slots. Invocation supplies the intended post-cost lifespan loss to Lifecycle's guarded response; Physiology supplies a survival guard and an opaque receipt for any intentional fatigue edit. The final composition rejects foreign age or body edits before session or costs publish. Recall-loss reconciliation guards the current route map, location, and pending legs; Transport stages the admitted native route replacement in that same batch.

Ordinary goods return no Record effect, and an already-composed record transfer needs no receipt write. Cargo keeps its complete ordered-prefix preflight and validates the final shared batch; declined transfers publish their failure event without an empty component batch.

Run `cargo bench -p game-invocation --bench transfers -- 200000 1000` to measure ordered Water transfer commits with all requests granted or half denied, alongside unrelated native records. Preparation and commit times are reported separately. This local benchmark does not measure the complete simulation; use `game-assembly`'s `full_world` benchmark for composed workload profiles.

On commit `081893a`, three paired release runs with 200,000 requests and 1,000 unrelated records reduced median commit time from 152.822 to 95.166 ms with all requests granted, and 86.680 to 54.002 ms with half denied. The matched 12-hour, 100-person water workload had overlapping timings: 13.532 s baseline median and 13.390 s optimized. All three saves matched exactly. This establishes a local transfer improvement; it does not establish a whole-game speedup or explain the earlier water regression.
