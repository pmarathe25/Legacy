# game-vitality

Inherited constitution and longevity set individual health capacity and natural lifespan. Speed, strength, endurance, agility, intelligence, and learning express their inherited potential through current age and condition. Aging begins halfway through the individual's natural lifespan; physical and cognitive effects differ. Guardians do not age.

Physical well-being reflects bodily health, hunger, and fatigue supplied by physiology. Stress and satisfaction reflect actual experiences and gradually return toward neutral. Inherited resilience changes the impact of stressful events; current stress or satisfaction is not inherited. These are small simulation rules, not a medical model.

Birth begins gradual development, with separate trajectories for body size, strength, endurance, locomotion, language readiness, reasoning, foresight, learning, and emotional regulation. Potential remains inherited; expressed childhood capabilities are smaller, and learning can peak before adulthood. These are tunable simulation approximations. Guardians retain mature expression.

Time spent in poor physical condition or distress during childhood leaves bounded acquired effects on later capability, patience, risk appetite, and sociability. Current condition can recover while that history remains. Accumulation uses elapsed time rather than the number of care reviews or experiences; registering an adult does not invent an earlier childhood. `Assess` exposes current traits, developmental fractions, and formative conditions separately from potential.

The generated campaign enables development. Small feature fixtures can omit this plugin and keep their existing baselines. Physiology owns health and death; transport owns travel; conflict owns resolved hits. This owner supplies characteristics and condition through read-only queries and typed requests.

Body growth preserves the health fraction rather than granting healing. Infants need carrying before independent walking and cannot participate in adult conversations before language readiness.
